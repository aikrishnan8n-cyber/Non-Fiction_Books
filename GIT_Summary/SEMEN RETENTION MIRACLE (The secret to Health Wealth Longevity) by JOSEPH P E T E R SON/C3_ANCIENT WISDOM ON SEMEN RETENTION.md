## 8.3 Important historical correction: Emerald Tablet and the seven principles

There is a significant historical problem in the author's explanation.

He states that the Emerald Tablet lists seven alchemical principles, identifying vibration as the third and rhythm as the fifth.

That attribution is historically inaccurate.

The familiar list of seven Hermetic principles, including vibration and rhythm, comes from *The Kybalion*, published in 1908 under the name Three Initiates.

The Emerald Tablet is a different, much shorter Hermetic-alchemical text.

Research documented by Oxford's Cabinet project traces the Emerald Tablet to an Arabic textual tradition, probably between the seventh and ninth centuries, rather than to the Hellenistic period as the book suggests. [cabinet.ox.ac.uk](https://www.cabinet.ox.ac.uk/emerald-tablet-hermes-trismegistus?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### Why is this important?

Because the author uses the historical attribution to establish the ancient origin of his argument.

The underlying philosophical ideas can still be discussed, but their historical sources should be correctly identified.

| Claim in the book | Historical clarification |
|---|---|
| Emerald Tablet contains seven principles | The familiar seven-principle system is from *The Kybalion*. |
| Vibration is the third principle | Correct within the *Kybalion* system. |
| Rhythm is the fifth principle | Correct within the *Kybalion* system. |
| Emerald Tablet originated in the Hellenistic era | Its known textual history points to a much later Arabic tradition. |
| Hermes Trismegistus physically authored the text | This is a traditional attribution, not established authorship. |

This is one of the most important historical corrections in the book.

### Important distinction

| Author's interpretation | Established biological understanding |
|---|---|
| Semen contains phosphorus. | True. |
| The body needs phosphorus. | True. |

| Retaining semen redirects phosphorus to the brain. | Not established. |

# 24. CHAPTER 3 — 25 IMPORTANT QUESTIONS AND DETAILED ANSWERS

### Q1. What is the central message of Chapter 3?

The central message is that sexual energy can be controlled and redirected towards other aspects of life. The author uses Chinese, Indian and Western philosophical traditions to explain why conserving sexual energy has historically been considered valuable.

### Q2. What is Qi?

Qi is a traditional Chinese concept associated with vital energy, breath and life processes. In the book, it is described as a force circulating throughout the body and supporting well-being.

### Q3. What is Prana?

Prana is a Sanskrit concept associated with breath, life and vitality. The author connects it with breathing, spiritual awareness and the energy believed to sustain human life.

### Q4. Are Qi and Prana identical?

No. They have some conceptual similarities, but they belong to different philosophical and cultural traditions. Their meanings and traditional applications are not identical.

### Q5. What is the subtle body?

The subtle body is a spiritual framework used in certain Indian and other philosophical traditions. It includes concepts such as chakras, energy pathways and Kundalini.

### Q6. What are chakras?

Chakras are traditionally described as energy centres within the subtle body. Different Indian spiritual traditions describe different chakra systems.

### Q7. What is a meridian?

A meridian is an energy pathway within traditional Chinese medicine through which Qi is believed to circulate. It is not an established anatomical structure.

### Q8. What is Kundalini?

Kundalini is a spiritual concept describing dormant transformative energy traditionally associated with the base of the spine. Its awakening is linked with spiritual development.

### Q9. What is the root chakra?

The root chakra, Muladhara, is traditionally associated with the base of the spine. The author connects it with foundational vitality, sexual energy and Kundalini.

### Q10. What is the crown chakra?

The crown chakra, Sahasrara, is associated with the top of the head. In the book, it represents spiritual awakening and the realization of universal consciousness.

### Q11. What is transmutation?

Transmutation means changing something from one form or condition into another. The author applies it to sexual energy, suggesting it can be redirected towards creativity, discipline and personal development.

### Q12. What is alchemy?

Alchemy is a historical tradition involving the transformation of substances. The author uses its symbolism to explain inner and personal transformation.

### Q13. Who is Hermes Trismegistus?

Hermes Trismegistus is a legendary figure associated with Hermetic philosophy. He combines characteristics of the Greek god Hermes and the Egyptian god Thoth.

### Q14. What is the Emerald Tablet?

It is a short Hermetic-alchemical text traditionally attributed to Hermes Trismegistus. It became influential in the history of Western alchemy.

### Q15. Does the Emerald Tablet contain the seven Hermetic principles?

No. The familiar seven-principle system comes from *The Kybalion*, published in 1908. The author incorrectly attributes that system to the Emerald Tablet.

The author describes it as the belief that everything has a vibrational frequency and that changes in frequency are associated with transformation. His application to sexual energy is philosophical rather than scientifically demonstrated.

### Q17. What is the principle of rhythm?

The author describes rhythm as the recurring cycles governing the universe and life. He encourages readers to align their behaviour with natural and biological rhythms.

### Q18. What did Pythagoras allegedly teach about semen?

The author presents Pythagoras as believing that semen was connected with the brain and creative energy. He uses this historical idea to support semen conservation.

### Q19. Who was Alcmaeon?

Alcmaeon was an ancient Greek medical thinker and philosopher. Historical reports associate him with the theory that semen originated in the brain, although the precise interpretation is disputed.

### Q20. Why does the author discuss phosphorus?

He argues that phosphorus is present in semen and important for the brain. He uses this to suggest that semen retention preserves a substance required for creativity. The conclusion that retention increases phosphorus available to the brain is not scientifically established.

### Q21. What does the philosopher's stone represent?

In the book, it represents the possibility of transforming something ordinary into something more valuable. The author uses it as a metaphor for transforming sexual impulses into productive motivation.

### Q22. What does Tao mean?

Tao generally means the Way. It is a central concept in Taoist philosophy associated with the natural order and flow of existence.

The author proposes that sexual desire can be transformed into love and broader passion for life. He argues that this motivation can be used for relationships, health, work and justice.

Veerya is a traditional Ayurvedic concept associated with vital essence. The author compares it with the essence of a tree that nourishes and supports the entire organism.

The final lesson is that people should develop conscious control over their desires and use their motivation to achieve meaningful goals.
