# 2. CLAIM 1 — FREQUENT EJACULATION PREVENTS PROSTATE CANCER

This is the first and most medically significant argument.

## 2.1 What is the prostate gland?

The prostate is a small reproductive gland in men.

It is located below the urinary bladder and surrounds part of the urethra.

Its main functions include:

- Producing fluid that contributes to semen.
- Supporting sperm function.
- Contributing to ejaculation.
- Participating in reproductive processes.

Prostate health becomes increasingly important as men grow older.

## 2.2 What is prostate cancer?

Prostate cancer occurs when cells in the prostate develop abnormal growth.

Some prostate cancers grow very slowly, while others can be aggressive.

Potential risk factors include:

- Increasing age.
- Family history.
- Inherited genetic factors.
- Certain population-related risk differences.

Not every person with prostate cancer experiences symptoms in its early stages.

# 4. THE 2016 PROSTATE CANCER STUDY — WHAT DID RESEARCHERS FIND?

This is particularly important because the author uses this study in his discussion.

### Study identification

**Title:** Ejaculation Frequency and Risk of Prostate Cancer: Updated Results with an Additional Decade of Follow-up.

**Year:** 2016.

**Research population:** Approximately 31,925 men.

**Research subject:** Relationship between ejaculation frequency and subsequent prostate cancer diagnosis.

The study was published in the *European Urology* journal.

The researchers examined whether ejaculation frequency was associated with prostate cancer risk.

### Findings

The researchers found an association between higher ejaculation frequency and lower prostate cancer incidence.

For men reporting at least 21 ejaculations per month, compared with men reporting 4–7 per month:

| Period assessed | Relative risk reported |
|---|---:|
| Ages 20–29 | 0.81 |
| Ages 40–49 | 0.78 |
| During 1991 | 0.75 |
| Lifetime average | 0.66 |

These results indicate lower relative incidence in the higher-frequency group in the study.

Importantly, the association was driven mainly by low-risk and organ-confined prostate cancer. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27033442/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## 4.1 What does relative risk 0.66 mean?

A relative risk of 0.66 represents approximately 34% lower relative risk compared with the reference group.

It does not mean that every individual who ejaculates 21 times monthly receives a 34% reduction in their personal cancer risk.

It is an association observed between groups in an observational study.

## 4.2 Does this prove that ejaculation prevents prostate cancer?

No.

This is where we need to understand an important scientific concept.

### Correlation versus causation

**Correlation** means two variables are associated.

**Causation** means one variable directly produces a change in another.

For example:

Imagine researchers find that people who regularly exercise have lower rates of certain diseases.

That does not automatically prove exercise is the only reason for the difference.

Other factors might influence both exercise habits and health.

The same reasoning applies to ejaculation and prostate cancer.

The 2016 research does not prove that increasing ejaculation frequency prevents prostate cancer.

Furthermore, the 2016 publication was an updated analysis of the Health Professionals Follow-Up Study, which had previously produced a related 2004 analysis. It should not be interpreted as an independent clinical trial. [JAMA Network](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/198487?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### My assessment

Peterson is right to challenge the idea that frequent ejaculation is a medically mandatory cancer-prevention measure.

However, describing the evidence as simply one unreplicated study is an incomplete summary of the research history.

# 5. DOES RETAINING SEMEN DAMAGE THE PROSTATE?

This is another important argument in Chapter 10.

## 5.1 The stagnation theory

Some critics suggest that semen retention causes sperm to accumulate.

They argue that:

1. Sperm remain inside the reproductive system.
2. Unused sperm accumulate.
3. The reproductive system becomes congested.
4. This creates pressure or inflammation.
5. The prostate may become damaged.

Peterson rejects this theory.

## 5.2 What actually happens to unused sperm?

The human body continuously produces sperm after puberty.

However, it does not store every sperm cell indefinitely.

Unused sperm eventually die and are broken down and reabsorbed by the body.

This is a normal physiological process.

Cleveland Clinic explains that sperm which are not released through ejaculation are naturally absorbed by the body. It also states that not ejaculating does not ordinarily cause harm. [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/sperm?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### Understand through an example

Imagine a system that continuously produces a substance.

The substance has several possible outcomes:

- It may be used.
- It may be released.
- It may be broken down and recycled.

The body has natural processes for managing unused sperm.

Therefore, sperm do not accumulate endlessly merely because someone does not ejaculate.

## 5.3 Does this prove semen retention improves health?

No.

It establishes that the body can manage unused sperm.

It does not prove that retaining semen increases energy, improves intelligence or prevents disease.

There is an important difference between:

- A biological process being harmless.
- That process producing additional health benefits.

They are not the same.

# 6. DOES EJACULATION FREQUENCY AFFECT LONGEVITY?

The author also discusses research suggesting that men with lower ejaculation frequency may have higher premature mortality.

He argues that this association may be explained by other health problems.

For example:

An individual may have a low ejaculation frequency because of:

- Existing illness.
- Reduced physical health.
- Medication.
- Depression.
- Hormonal problems.
- Reduced libido.

In that situation, poor health may contribute to both reduced sexual activity and increased mortality risk.

It would therefore be incorrect to conclude that reduced ejaculation itself caused the higher mortality.

### Important lesson

A person's sexual activity is influenced by general health.

This makes observational studies difficult to interpret.

We need to distinguish between:

**Ejaculation frequency causing a health outcome**

and

**A health condition affecting ejaculation frequency and the outcome simultaneously.**

These are different explanations.

# 7. DOES EJACULATION LOWER TESTOSTERONE AND PREVENT PROSTATE CANCER?

The author discusses a theory that frequent ejaculation reduces testosterone and thereby lowers prostate cancer risk.

This explanation is problematic.

Testosterone is involved in prostate biology, but the relationship between testosterone, prostate cancer and treatment is more complicated than a simple assumption that lower testosterone always means lower cancer risk.

The 2016 ejaculation-frequency study did not establish that ejaculation prevents cancer by lowering testosterone.

Also, normal changes in testosterone following sexual activity should not be confused with sustained reductions in testosterone.

### Main takeaway

Neither ejaculation frequency nor semen retention should be used as a way to manipulate testosterone for prostate cancer prevention.

For prostate health, proper medical assessment and appropriate screening discussions are more important.

The American Cancer Society's updated 2026 guidance focuses on informed decisions about prostate-specific antigen (PSA) screening, taking age and individual risk into account.

It does not prescribe ejaculation frequency as a cancer-prevention treatment. [American Cancer Society](https://www.cancer.org/cancer/types/prostate-cancer/detection-diagnosis-staging/acs-recommendations.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

For prostate health, the more appropriate approach is to:

- Understand your family history.
- Discuss relevant risk factors with a healthcare professional.
- Seek medical evaluation for concerning urinary or prostate symptoms.
- Discuss whether PSA screening is appropriate for your age and risk.

Do not rely on semen retention or frequent ejaculation as a substitute for medical care.

## 9.2 Is choosing not to ejaculate a sexual disorder?

No. Voluntarily choosing not to ejaculate is not, by itself, a sexual disorder.

This distinction is important.

Modern medical guidance defines delayed ejaculation in relation to difficulty or inability to ejaculate despite adequate sexual stimulation and a desire to ejaculate.

The 2026 American Urological Association/Sexual Medicine Society of North America guideline describes delayed ejaculation as a consistent, bothersome inability to ejaculate despite strong desire and adequate stimulation. The key difference is intentional choice versus unwanted inability.

### Compare these situations

**Situation A — Voluntary semen retention**

A person experiences sexual desire and has the ability to ejaculate.

However, they deliberately choose not to ejaculate because of personal or spiritual preferences.

This is not automatically a disorder.

**Situation B — Delayed ejaculation**

A person wants to ejaculate and receives adequate sexual stimulation but repeatedly cannot do so, or experiences a clinically significant delay that causes distress.

This may warrant evaluation for delayed ejaculation.

**Situation C — Involuntary inability to ejaculate**

A person consistently cannot ejaculate despite wanting to.

This may have medical, neurological, medication-related or psychological causes.

It deserves appropriate evaluation.

- Conflict with their partner.

- Shame following ejaculation.

These are possible personal or behavioural difficulties, rather than evidence of a dangerous physical energy buildup.

**Physical harm from retained semen**

and

**Psychological discomfort associated with a strict abstinence commitment.**

The first is not established as a normal consequence of abstaining.

Traditional Taoist and Tantric practices have a long philosophical and spiritual history.

It does not independently establish all the modern health, hormonal, fertility and longevity claims made about semen retention.
