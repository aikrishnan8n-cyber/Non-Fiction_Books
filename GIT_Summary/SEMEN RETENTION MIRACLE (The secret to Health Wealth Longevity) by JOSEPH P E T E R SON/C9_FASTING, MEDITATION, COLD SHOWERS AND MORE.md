# 3. TECHNIQUE 2 — INTERPRET YOUR FEELINGS WITH WORDS

This is one of the most psychologically interesting techniques in the chapter.

## 3.1 What does the author mean?

Peterson explains that people sometimes respond to uncomfortable emotions by seeking temporary pleasure.

For example:

Someone feels anxious.

They do not understand why they feel anxious.

They seek something distracting or pleasurable.

That activity might involve sexual stimulation, junk food or excessive gaming.

The author believes that identifying emotions can help people identify their actual needs.

## 3.2 Understand the relationship between emotions and needs

Consider these examples:

| Emotion | Possible underlying need |
|---|---|
| Apprehension | Comfort or reassurance |
| Boredom | Stimulation or engagement |
| Loneliness | Connection |
| Stress | Relaxation |
| Frustration | A sense of progress |
| Sadness | Support |
| Insecurity | Reassurance |
| Exhaustion | Rest |

The same emotion can have different causes and needs for different individuals.

## 3.3 How to practise this technique

When an unwanted urge appears, ask yourself:

1. What am I feeling?
2. What happened immediately before this feeling?
3. What do I actually need?
4. Is there another healthy way to meet that need?

### Example

Suppose you feel sexually distracted while studying.

Instead of automatically reacting, examine the situation.

You discover that you are actually tired and bored.

You take a short break, drink water and go for a walk.

After returning, you feel more prepared to continue studying.

The important point is that the original need was rest and stimulation, not necessarily sexual activity.

### Scientific perspective

The psychological process of identifying emotions is known as *affect labelling*.

Research has examined whether naming emotions can help regulate emotional responses. However, findings are mixed: some studies report benefits, while others find that naming an emotion does not automatically reduce distress. [Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/1754073917742706?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

**Main lesson:** Understanding your emotional needs can help you make more deliberate choices instead of reacting automatically.

# 11. TECHNIQUE 10 — TRY FASTING

This is one of the more controversial recommendations in Chapter 9.

We need to distinguish the author's explanation from the research he cites.

## 11.1 What does the author claim?

Peterson argues that fasting can reduce certain hormones and may therefore influence sexual desire.

He discusses research involving men observing Ramadan.

According to his interpretation:

- FSH decreased.
- Sexual desire decreased.
- Body weight decreased.
- Sexual intercourse frequency decreased.

He suggests that fasting tells the body that food is limited, causing the body to prioritize survival rather than reproduction.

## 11.2 The study discussed by the author

The reference is:

**Talib et al. (2015)**  
*The Effect of Fasting on Erectile Function and Sexual Desire on Men in the Month of Ramadan.*

The study involved 45 men with an average age of approximately 37 years.

Researchers compared measurements before and after Ramadan.

### Findings

| Measurement | Finding |
|---|---|
| Sexual desire | Decreased significantly |
| Frequency of sexual intercourse | Decreased significantly |
| Body weight | Decreased significantly |
| FSH | Decreased significantly |
| Testosterone | No significant change |
| LH | No significant change |
| Erectile function | No significant change |
| Estradiol | No significant change |

The researchers concluded that Ramadan fasting might be associated with reduced sexual desire and frequency of intercourse. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25923156/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## 11.3 Does fasting reliably reduce testosterone?

No.

A 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis examined Ramadan fasting and hormonal levels among healthy adults.

It included 35 original studies with 1,107 participants.

The pooled analysis did not find significant overall changes in testosterone, LH or FSH. [PubMed Central (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11115274/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## 11.4 What is the correct conclusion?

Fasting may influence sexual desire in some circumstances.

But the evidence does not establish fasting as a reliable method of reducing libido or increasing semen-retention benefits.

Ramadan fasting also involves changes in meal timing, hydration, sleep and daily routine, so its findings cannot automatically be applied to every intermittent-fasting programme.

### Important practical caution

Do not undertake prolonged or restrictive fasting simply to suppress normal sexual desire.

Fasting should be appropriate for your health and circumstances. People with medical conditions, medication requirements or a history of disordered eating should seek appropriate medical advice before attempting restrictive fasting.

**My conclusion:** Fasting is not necessary for semen retention.

# 12. TECHNIQUE 11 — COLD SHOWERS

## 12.1 What does the author claim?

Peterson describes cold showers as a traditional way of reducing sexual excitement.

He explains that the autonomic nervous system has two major divisions:

1. Sympathetic nervous system.
2. Parasympathetic nervous system.

He describes the sympathetic system as the fight-or-flight system and the parasympathetic system as the relaxation system.

His explanation is that cold showers stimulate the vagus nerve and activate the parasympathetic nervous system, helping someone calm down.

## 12.2 Understanding the nervous systems

| System | General function |
|---|---|
| Sympathetic | Mobilizes the body during demanding situations |
| Parasympathetic | Supports recovery, digestion and other resting functions |
| Vagus nerve | Major nerve involved in parasympathetic regulation |

The author's explanation is a simplified version of a more complex physiological system.

## 12.3 What does modern research show?

A 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis examined cold-water immersion and other cold-exposure techniques.

It found changes in several heart-rate-variability measures consistent with increased parasympathetic activity after cold exposure.

However, the results depended on the exposure method and individual characteristics. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38663342/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

There is an important qualification.

The initial cold-shock response can activate the sympathetic nervous system, causing:

- Increased heart rate.
- Rapid breathing.
- Increased blood pressure.
- Peripheral vasoconstriction.

These effects are documented in a systematic review of cold-shock responses. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38211547/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### So is a cold shower a reliable way to suppress sexual urges?

That has not been established.

It may provide a temporary change in sensation, alertness or attention, but it should not be regarded as a proven libido-control treatment.

### Practical recommendation

A comfortably cool shower is sufficient if you enjoy it.

You do not need extreme cold exposure or ice baths.

People with cardiovascular or other relevant health conditions should be cautious with sudden intense cold exposure.

# 21. CHAPTER 9 — IMPORTANT SCIENTIFIC AND CRITICAL ANALYSIS

There are four major areas where the author requires additional qualification.

## 21.1 Fasting does not reliably reduce testosterone

The Ramadan study found reduced sexual desire and FSH in a small group, but no significant testosterone reduction.

The 2024 meta-analysis also did not find a significant pooled change in major male reproductive hormones.

Therefore, fasting should not be considered a reliable hormonal method of controlling libido. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25923156/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## 21.2 Cold showers do not simply activate relaxation

Cold exposure produces complex physiological responses.

The immediate cold-shock response may increase sympathetic activity.

Some later cardiovascular measurements can reflect greater parasympathetic activity.

Therefore, the author's simple explanation that cold showers directly activate relaxation through the vagus nerve is incomplete. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38211547/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## 21.3 Specific foods are not proven libido suppressants

The chapter lists several foods that allegedly increase or decrease sexual desire.

However, clinical evidence supporting these specific claims is insufficient.

A balanced diet is much more important for general health. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19796015/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## 21.4 Meditation has more defensible benefits

The chapter's discussion of meditation is more credible.

Research supports possible improvements in anxiety, depression and psychological stress.

However, meditation should not be presented as a treatment for semen-related problems or as a guaranteed method of controlling libido. [JAMA Network](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/1809754?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

# 24. CHAPTER 9 — 30 IMPORTANT QUESTIONS AND DETAILED ANSWERS

### Q1. What is the main purpose of Chapter 9?

The chapter introduces practical activities that Peterson believes can help individuals manage sexual urges and redirect their attention towards more productive areas of life.

### Q2. Why is self-awareness important?

Self-awareness helps individuals understand their thoughts, emotions, triggers and behaviours. It allows them to identify why unwanted urges arise rather than responding automatically.

### Q3. What is a sexual trigger?

A sexual trigger is a situation, thought, emotion or environmental stimulus that produces sexual excitement or unwanted sexual behaviour.

### Q4. How can someone identify personal triggers?

By observing situations, emotional states, daily routines and environmental factors associated with repeated urges. Journaling can help identify patterns.

### Q5. What does interpreting feelings with words mean?

It means identifying and naming emotions, such as boredom, loneliness, apprehension or frustration, and examining the needs associated with them.

### Q6. What is affect labelling?

Affect labelling is the psychological process of expressing emotional experiences using words. It has been studied as a possible method of emotional regulation, although research findings are mixed.

### Q7. What does the author mean by setting thoughts aside?

He suggests acknowledging sexual thoughts without automatically acting on them. The objective is to return attention to another activity without feeling shame.

### Q8. Why does the author recommend taking breaks?

He believes fatigue and boredom can encourage unwanted thoughts. Walking, resting and eating a healthy snack can provide a mental reset.

### Q9. What is the purpose of journaling?

Journaling helps individuals record thoughts, identify triggers, understand their emotions and clarify personal goals.

### Q10. How does music help?

The author uses music as a distraction and mood-changing activity. Music can encourage dancing, relaxation and physical movement.

### Q11. Why does the author recommend phoning a friend?

He believes social support helps individuals manage difficult feelings, receive encouragement and address emotional needs such as loneliness.

Exercise supports physical fitness, can improve mood and provides a constructive activity for redirecting attention.

He suggests fasting may influence certain hormones and sexual desire. However, scientific evidence does not establish fasting as a reliable libido-control method.

There was no statistically significant difference in testosterone levels before and after Ramadan. Sexual desire and FSH levels decreased significantly.

It is the division of the autonomic nervous system involved in mobilizing the body during demanding situations.

### Q18. What is the parasympathetic nervous system?

It is the division of the autonomic nervous system associated with resting, digestion and recovery functions.

### Q19. What is the vagus nerve?

It is a major nerve involved in parasympathetic regulation, connecting the brain with several organs.

### Q20. Do cold showers directly suppress sexual desire?

There is insufficient evidence to establish this. Cold exposure produces several autonomic responses, but it is not a proven treatment for reducing libido.

Meditation develops awareness and attention. It can help individuals observe thoughts and emotions without automatically reacting to them.

He argues that unstructured time can encourage boredom and distracting thoughts. A balanced schedule provides more meaningful activities.

It is an attention-interruption technique in which someone deliberately thinks about something unrelated and unattractive to interrupt a sexual thought.

An aphrodisiac is a substance traditionally believed to increase sexual desire or performance.

An anaphrodisiac is a substance traditionally believed to reduce sexual desire. The book lists several foods, but their claimed effects are not scientifically established.

He believes observing food consumption alongside sexual desire may help someone identify possible personal associations.

It demonstrates how to balance meditation, journaling, exercise, work, social activities, reading, creativity and sleep.

Several techniques have general-health support, particularly exercise and meditation. However, none should be considered a guaranteed way to increase the specific benefits of semen retention.

The main lesson is to understand your triggers, manage your emotions, create healthier routines and redirect attention towards activities that support your personal goals.
