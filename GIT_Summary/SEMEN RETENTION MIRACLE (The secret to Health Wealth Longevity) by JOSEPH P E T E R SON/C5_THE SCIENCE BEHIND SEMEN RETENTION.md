# 2. UNDERSTANDING SIX IMPORTANT SCIENTIFIC MEASUREMENTS

Before examining the studies, we need to understand the measurements researchers use.

These are essential terms in male reproductive research.

## 2.1 Semen volume

Semen volume measures how much seminal fluid is released during ejaculation.

It is normally measured in millilitres (mL).

For example:

A sample containing 3 mL of semen has a volume of 3 millilitres.

## 2.2 Sperm concentration

Sperm concentration measures the number of sperm cells in each millilitre of semen.

It is commonly expressed as millions of sperm per millilitre.

Example:

A sample has a concentration of 40 million sperm/mL.

This means each millilitre contains approximately 40 million sperm cells.

## 2.3 Total sperm count

Total sperm count measures the total number of sperm cells in the entire ejaculate.

Formula:

**Total sperm count = Semen volume × Sperm concentration**

Example:

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---:|
| Semen volume | 3 mL |
| Sperm concentration | 40 million/mL |
| Total sperm count | 120 million |

Calculation:

3 × 40 = 120 million sperm.

## 2.4 Sperm motility

Motility refers to the ability of sperm to move.

Movement is important because sperm must travel through the female reproductive tract to reach the egg.

There are two important categories:

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Progressive motility | Sperm move forward in a purposeful direction. |
| Non-progressive motility | Sperm move, but without effective forward progression. |

Immotile sperm are sperm that do not move.

## 2.5 Sperm viability

Viability measures the percentage of living sperm cells.

A sperm cell may be alive but not necessarily capable of moving properly.

Therefore, viability and motility are different measurements.

## 2.6 Sperm morphology

Morphology refers to the physical shape and structure of sperm.

A normal sperm generally has:

- An appropriately formed head.
- A properly developed midpiece.
- A suitable tail.

Abnormalities in these structures can affect sperm function.

### Complete comparison

| Measurement | What it measures | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Semen volume | Quantity of seminal fluid | Helps assess semen characteristics |
| Concentration | Sperm per millilitre | Measures sperm density |
| Total count | Total sperm in ejaculate | Measures total sperm output |
| Motility | Movement ability | Important for reaching the egg |
| Viability | Percentage of living sperm | Indicates sperm survival |
| Morphology | Shape and structure | Evaluates physical characteristics |

These measurements form the foundation for understanding the research discussed in this chapter.

# 3. CONCEPT 1 — EFFECT OF ABSTINENCE ON SEMEN VOLUME

## 3.1 What does the author claim?

The author explains that semen volume tends to increase following periods of sexual abstinence.

He references the review by Ayad, Van der Horst and Du Plessis (2018).

According to his summary:

- 23 out of 24 studies found increased semen volume following abstinence.
- One study found no statistically significant change.
- Several studies also reported increased testosterone following longer abstinence.

He suggests that increased testosterone may explain the increase in semen volume.



## 3.2 Understanding the research

The paper is:

*Revisiting the Relationship between the Ejaculatory Abstinence Period and Semen Characteristics.*

Authors:
- Bashir M. Ayad.
- Gerhard Van der Horst.
- Stefan S. Du Plessis.

Published in 2018 in the *International Journal of Fertility and Sterility*.

The review considered 30 studies examining relationships between abstinence duration and semen characteristics. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29043697/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### Why might semen volume increase?

When ejaculation does not occur for a period:

- More time is available for reproductive gland secretions to accumulate.
- Sperm can accumulate in the reproductive ducts.
- The next ejaculate may contain a greater volume.

This is a plausible physiological explanation.

However, greater volume does not necessarily mean better sperm function.

### Important scientific correction: WHO semen-volume reference

The author describes 1.5 mL as the lowest WHO level for healthy semen.

That figure came from the WHO fifth edition (2010).

The WHO sixth edition (2021) reports a lower fifth-percentile reference value of 1.4 mL.

More importantly, these numbers are reference limits derived from men whose partners achieved pregnancy within 12 months. They are not absolute boundaries between fertility and infertility.

The American Urological Association also emphasizes that individual semen parameters cannot independently establish whether a man is fertile or infertile. [PubMed Central (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10929669/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### Main lesson

Longer abstinence can increase semen volume.

But higher semen volume alone does not establish better fertility.

# 4. CONCEPT 2 — SPERM CONCENTRATION AND TOTAL COUNT

## 4.1 What does the author claim?

The author reports that:

- 22 studies examined sperm concentration.
- 20 of these studies found a significant increase following prolonged abstinence.
- The greatest increase generally occurred during the first two to five days.
- Smaller increases were also reported with longer abstinence periods.

He also discusses studies showing increased total sperm count in ejaculates following abstinence.



## 4.2 Why does sperm concentration matter?

Imagine two semen samples.

| Parameter | Sample A | Sample B |
|---|---:|---:|
| Semen volume | 2 mL | 4 mL |
| Sperm concentration | 50 million/mL | 50 million/mL |
| Total sperm count | 100 million | 200 million |

Both samples have identical sperm concentration.

However, Sample B has twice the total sperm count because its semen volume is higher.

This demonstrates why researchers examine both concentration and total sperm count.

## 4.3 Why might abstinence increase total sperm count?

Sperm production is continuous.

However, the number of sperm available in the reproductive ducts can vary with ejaculation frequency.

A longer interval may allow more sperm to accumulate before the next ejaculation.

Therefore, the next sample may contain more sperm.

### Scientific assessment

This is one of the more credible findings discussed in the book.

Modern research continues to find that longer abstinence generally increases semen volume and total sperm count.

However, that does not mean longer abstinence improves every other sperm characteristic.

A 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis involving 82 studies found that longer abstinence intervals were associated with higher semen volume and total sperm count. It also found that shorter intervals were associated with better total and progressive sperm motility. The certainty of the evidence was low or very low. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42972349/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### Main lesson

Longer abstinence can increase the quantity of sperm present in a particular ejaculate.

It does not automatically improve all aspects of sperm quality.

# 5. CONCEPT 3 — SPERM MOTILITY AND KINEMATICS

Motility refers to the ability of sperm to move.

He reports:

- Eight reported an increase in the total number of motile sperm.
- Four found no significant difference.

He further explains that the number of motile sperm initially increased during abstinence.

## 5.3 Progressive motility

The author separately examines progressive motility.

## 5.4 Why are the results important?

Consider two hypothetical samples.

| Parameter | Sample A | Sample B |
|---|---:|---:|
| Total sperm count | 200 million | 100 million |
| Progressive motility | 30% | 50% |
| Progressive motile sperm | 60 million | 50 million |

Sample A has more total sperm.

Sample B has a higher progressive-motility percentage.

Neither measurement should be evaluated in isolation.

This is why sperm concentration and motility must be considered together.

## 5.5 What does newer research show?

The 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis found that shorter abstinence intervals were associated with approximately:

- 4.37 percentage points higher total sperm motility.
- 3.24 percentage points higher progressive sperm motility.

Longer intervals, meanwhile, were associated with higher semen volume and total sperm count.

The researchers also emphasized that the evidence had considerable heterogeneity and low or very low certainty. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42972349/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### Main lesson

There is a trade-off between quantity and movement characteristics.

More sperm in an ejaculate does not necessarily mean that those sperm are more effective at fertilization.

Viability means the percentage of sperm cells that are alive.

It is different from motility.

For example:

- A living sperm cell may be unable to move.

### Main lesson

Living sperm, moving sperm and genetically intact sperm are different things.

A semen sample should be evaluated using multiple parameters.

### Main lesson

# 12. STUDY 2 — JIANG ET AL. (2003)

This is one of the most important references in the entire book because the author uses it to support the famous 145.7% testosterone increase.

**Title:** A research on the relationship between ejaculation and serum testosterone level in men.

**Authors:** Ming Jiang and colleagues.

**Year:** 2003.

**Participants reported in the paper:** 28 men.

## 12.2 What did the study report?

The retraction notice is available through Springer. [Springer Link](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1631/jzus.2003.r236.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### Why does this matter?

Because many semen-retention discussions repeatedly quote the 145.7% figure without mentioning the retraction.

A retracted paper should not be treated as reliable evidence for this claim.

### Important conclusion

**The famous claim that testosterone rises by 45.7% on the seventh day of semen retention is not established by reliable evidence from this study.**

Do not use this number as a medically proven prediction.

# 15. CONCEPT 8 — ANDROGEN RECEPTORS AND ANIMAL RESEARCH

Androgen receptors are cellular receptors that bind hormones such as testosterone.

Androgen receptors are involved in several functions, including:

- Male reproductive development.
- Sexual function.
- Muscle biology.
- Bone biology.
- Certain brain functions.

They are not the same as ordinary neurotransmitter receptors at a synapse.

This is an important scientific distinction.

## 15.2 What did the author claim?

## 15.3 What did the actual research investigate?

*Relationship between Sexual Satiety and Brain Androgen Receptors.*

Published in *Neuroendocrinology* in 2007.

Researchers studied male rats after sexual satiety.

The animals were allowed to engage in sexual activity for four hours.

Researchers then examined androgen-receptor density at different time intervals.

### Findings

- Some animals displayed reduced sexual behaviour.
- Reduced androgen-receptor density was observed in a specific brain region.

At 72 hours:

- Sexual activity recovered.
- Androgen-receptor density returned to control levels in one region.
- Increased receptor expression was observed in several other regions.

Importantly, serum testosterone levels did not change during the post-satiety period. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17268169/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## 15.4 Why is this important?

The book's interpretation is stronger than what the study establishes.

The study involved:

- Rats, not humans.
- Sexual satiety, not a human semen-retention challenge.
- Specific brain regions, not the entire brain.
- Temporary receptor changes, not proof of improved human testosterone utilization.

### My assessment

This is interesting animal research.

But it does not prove that human semen retention increases androgen-receptor efficiency or improves testosterone function.

# 33. CHAPTER 5 — 25 IMPORTANT QUESTIONS AND DETAILED ANSWERS

### Q1. What is the central purpose of Chapter 5?

The chapter aims to examine scientific evidence relating to semen retention, particularly changes in semen quality and testosterone levels. The author attempts to establish that abstinence can have physical and reproductive benefits.

### Q2. What is semen volume?

Semen volume is the quantity of fluid released during ejaculation, measured in millilitres. It includes secretions from different reproductive glands and is not the same as sperm count.

### Q3. What is sperm concentration?

It is the number of sperm cells present in each millilitre of semen. It is commonly measured in millions per millilitre.

### Q4. What is total sperm count?

It is the total number of sperm in the entire ejaculate. It is calculated by multiplying semen volume by sperm concentration.

### Q5. Why does semen volume increase with abstinence?

Longer abstinence gives more time for reproductive gland secretions and sperm to accumulate before the next ejaculation.

### Q6. What did Ayad et al. (2018) investigate?

They reviewed scientific research examining the relationship between ejaculatory abstinence and semen characteristics, including volume, concentration, motility, viability and morphology.

### Q7. What is sperm motility?

Motility refers to the ability of sperm to move. Progressive motility is particularly important because it measures purposeful forward movement.

### Q8. What is the difference between motility and viability?

Motility measures movement, while viability measures whether sperm cells are alive. A living sperm cell may not necessarily move properly.

### Q9. What is sperm morphology?

Morphology is the study of sperm shape and structure. Researchers examine the head, midpiece and tail to determine whether sperm have normal physical characteristics.

### Q10. What is DNA fragmentation?

DNA fragmentation refers to damage or breaks in the genetic material contained in sperm. Excessive fragmentation may affect reproductive outcomes.

### Q11. What are reactive oxygen species?

ROS are reactive oxygen-containing molecules produced through normal cellular processes. Excessive quantities can cause oxidative stress and potentially damage sperm cells.

### Q12. What are seminal plasma antioxidants?

They are substances present in seminal fluid that help control oxidative processes and protect sperm against excessive oxidative damage.

### Q13. Does prolonged abstinence always improve sperm quality?

No. Longer abstinence often increases semen volume and total sperm count, but shorter periods may produce better motility and lower DNA fragmentation in some studies.

### Q14. What did Exton et al. (2001) investigate?

They investigated hormonal and cardiovascular responses to masturbation-induced orgasm in ten healthy men before and after three weeks of sexual abstinence.

### Q15. What did the Exton study find?

Orgasm increased cardiovascular activity, prolactin and catecholamines. Testosterone did not change in response to orgasm itself, although higher concentrations were measured after the abstinence period.

### Q16. What was the famous Jiang et al. (2003) finding?

The study reported that testosterone reached 145.7% of baseline on the seventh day of abstinence. However, the study was retracted in 2021 and should not be used as reliable supporting evidence.

### Q17. What did Sztajzel et al. (2000) investigate?

They examined the effect of sexual activity on exercise performance, testosterone and concentration in 15 high-level male athletes.

### Q18. Did sexual activity reduce maximum athletic performance in that study?

No. The researchers found no detrimental effect on maximum workload or mental concentration. Some heart-rate recovery measurements were temporarily higher following sexual activity approximately two hours earlier.

### Q19. What are androgen receptors?

Androgen receptors are cellular receptors that bind testosterone and other androgens. They influence gene expression and participate in reproductive, muscular and other biological functions.

### Q20. What did the 2007 animal study find?

It found temporary changes in androgen-receptor density in selected brain regions of male rats following sexual satiety. Serum testosterone did not change during the post-satiety period.

### Q21. Why is the animal study not proof of human semen-retention benefits?

Because it investigated rats, specific brain regions and temporary responses following sexual satiety. It did not establish that human semen retention improves androgen-receptor functioning.

### Q22. What was Brown-Séquard's historical experiment?

In 1889, Brown-Séquard injected himself with extracts from animal testicles and reported subjective physical and cognitive improvements. This was an uncontrolled historical self-experiment rather than modern clinical evidence.

### Q23. What physical functions are associated with testosterone?

Testosterone contributes to bone health, muscle development, reproductive function, sexual characteristics and other physiological processes. Its effects depend on the individual's hormonal status and overall health.

### Q24. Does evidence supporting testosterone therapy prove that semen retention improves health?

No. Testosterone replacement studies investigate a medical intervention, often in men with low testosterone. They do not demonstrate that abstinence produces equivalent hormonal changes or clinical benefits.

### Q25. What is the final scientific lesson from Chapter 5?

Abstinence duration can influence particular semen parameters, but the effects differ between volume, count, motility and DNA integrity. Evidence does not establish that semen retention consistently improves testosterone, fertility or general health.
