# 2. CONCEPT 1 — HOW TO AVOID EJACULATION

This is the first practical technique introduced by the author.

He explains that pelvic-floor muscle awareness is important for learning control over sexual responses.

## 2.1 What are pelvic-floor muscles?

The pelvic floor is a group of muscles and connective tissues located at the bottom of the pelvis.

These muscles support several bodily functions, including:

- Bladder control.
- Bowel control.
- Sexual function.
- Support of pelvic organs.
- Coordination during ejaculation.

In men, pelvic-floor muscles include structures involved in supporting the pelvic organs and controlling certain aspects of urinary and sexual function.

## 2.2 How does the author suggest identifying these muscles?

He provides two examples.

**Method 1: Stopping the urine stream**

Imagine that you are urinating and briefly try to stop the flow.

Notice the muscles involved.

**Method 2: Preventing the passage of gas**

Imagine that you are trying to prevent passing gas.

Notice which muscles contract.

These sensations help identify the pelvic-floor muscles.

The author refers to them as Kegel muscles.

### Important medical correction

Stopping urine midstream can help you recognize the muscles once, but it should not become your regular exercise technique.

Repeatedly interrupting urination can interfere with normal bladder emptying.

Medical guidance from NIDDK recommends identifying the muscles this way if necessary, then performing exercises separately when the bladder is empty. [NIDDK](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/urologic-diseases/kegel-exercises.?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

# 3. CONCEPT 2 — KEGEL EXERCISES

## 3.1 What are Kegel exercises?

Kegel exercises involve deliberately contracting and relaxing the pelvic-floor muscles.

They are commonly used to improve pelvic-floor strength and control.

The author recommends practising these contractions in different positions:

- Standing.
- Sitting.
- Walking.
- Lying down.

His main instruction is to contract the pelvic-floor muscles while keeping the buttocks, thighs and abdomen relaxed.

## 3.2 The author's suggested exercise

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Identify the pelvic-floor muscles. |
| 2 | Contract the muscles. |
| 3 | Keep the buttocks relaxed. |
| 4 | Keep the thighs relaxed. |
| 5 | Avoid tightening the abdomen. |
| 6 | Continue breathing normally. |
| 7 | Relax the muscles after contraction. |
| 8 | Repeat consistently. |

The author recommends ten sets of contractions at least three times daily.



## 3.3 What is the correct way to perform a Kegel?

A commonly recommended introductory routine for men is:

1. Sit or lie comfortably.
2. Identify the muscles used to prevent passing gas.
3. Gently contract and lift those muscles.
4. Hold for approximately 3–5 seconds, according to your ability.
5. Completely relax for a similar period.
6. Repeat several times.
7. Breathe normally throughout.

The exact number of repetitions should be adjusted to your ability and, when necessary, professional guidance.

Continence Health Australia's guidance recommends gradually building towards approximately 8–10 repetitions per set and three sets per day. [Continence Health Australia](https://www.continence.org.au/living-with-incontinence/pelvic-floor-exercises/pelvic-floor-exercises-for-men/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### Important: Contraction and relaxation are equally important

A common mistake is repeatedly tightening the pelvic floor without allowing it to relax.

This can create excessive muscular tension.

Some men with pelvic-floor problems need relaxation and coordination training rather than additional strengthening.

The American Urological Association's 2025 chronic pelvic pain guideline recognizes individualized pelvic-floor physical therapy, including relaxation and coordination training. [American Urological Association](https://www.auanet.org/documents/Guidelines/PDF/2025%20Guidelines/CPP%20Unabridged%20041025.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

**Main lesson:** Pelvic-floor control requires both the ability to contract and the ability to relax.

# 5. CONCEPT 4 — DRY ORGASM

## 5.1 What is a dry orgasm?

A dry orgasm refers to experiencing orgasm with little or no semen visibly expelled from the penis.

The author presents this as one of the central methods for combining sexual pleasure with semen retention.

However, there is an important medical distinction.

Orgasm and ejaculation are related but separate physiological events.

### Orgasm

Orgasm is the subjective neurological experience of sexual climax.

### Ejaculation

Ejaculation is the physical release of semen through the urethra.

They normally occur together, but they do not have to.

The American Urological Association recognizes them as distinct physiological processes that can occur independently. [auau.auanet.org](https://auau.auanet.org/sites/default/files/media/2023-08/Disorders%20of%20Ejaculation_%20An%20AUA_SMSNA%20Guideline%20%282020%29%20-%20American%20Urological%20Association.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## 5.2 Three different situations

| Situation | What happens |
|---|---|
| Normal ejaculation | Orgasm occurs and semen leaves through the penis. |
| Orgasm without ejaculation | Orgasm occurs without visible semen expulsion. |
| Retrograde ejaculation | Semen travels backwards into the bladder. |

These should not be confused.

# 6. CONCEPT 5 — RETROGRADE EJACULATION

This is a particularly important part of Chapter 8 because the author discusses applying pressure to the area between the scrotum and anus.

He claims that this can cause semen to enter the bladder instead of leaving through the penis.

There is a medical correction needed here.

## 6.1 What is retrograde ejaculation?

Retrograde ejaculation occurs when semen travels backwards into the urinary bladder instead of being expelled through the penis.

Normally, the bladder neck closes during ejaculation.

This prevents semen from entering the bladder.

When this mechanism does not function properly, semen can travel backwards.

### Common causes include:

- Certain medications.
- Diabetes-related nerve damage.
- Neurological conditions.
- Prostate or bladder-neck surgery.
- Other abnormalities affecting bladder-neck function.

A person may experience orgasm but release little or no visible semen.

Cloudy urine after orgasm is one possible symptom. [nhs.uk](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/Ejaculation-problems/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## 6.2 Is pressing the perineum a recommended method?

The book's suggestion that external pressure on the perineum deliberately produces retrograde ejaculation is not supported by the medical references examined here.

Retrograde ejaculation is associated with failure of the bladder neck to close properly, not simply with pressing the area between the scrotum and anus.

**Do not deliberately attempt to induce retrograde ejaculation as a semen-retention technique.**

It is a medical ejaculation disorder and can affect fertility.

### Main takeaway

A dry orgasm and retrograde ejaculation are not automatically the same thing.

A dry orgasm can have different underlying mechanisms.

# 26. IMPORTANT SAFETY AND PRACTICAL NOTES

There are five points worth remembering.

**1. Do not repeatedly stop urination to exercise pelvic-floor muscles.**

Use that action only to identify the muscles initially. Perform normal exercises separately. [NIDDK](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/urologic-diseases/kegel-exercises.?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

**2. Do not overtrain the pelvic floor.**

Excessive contractions can create tension or discomfort. Some people need relaxation training rather than strengthening.

**3. Do not deliberately try to produce retrograde ejaculation.**

It is not a proven semen-retention method and can be relevant to fertility problems. [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/21870-retrograde-ejaculation?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

**4. Do not treat dry orgasm as guaranteed.**

Orgasm and ejaculation can occur independently, but the ability to separate them varies between individuals.

**5. Maintain mutual consent and communication.**

Neither partner should feel pressured to avoid orgasm, ejaculation or any particular form of sexual activity.

The chapter teaches techniques that the author believes allow someone to maintain sexual intimacy while practising semen retention. It covers pelvic-floor awareness, Tantric sex, orgasm control and Karezza.

They are muscles and supporting tissues located at the bottom of the pelvis. They help support pelvic organs and participate in urinary and sexual functions.

Kegel exercises strengthen and improve awareness of pelvic-floor muscles through controlled contractions and relaxation.

He wants the individual to isolate the pelvic-floor muscles rather than unnecessarily tightening surrounding muscles.

### Q8. What role does breathing play?

Slow, comfortable breathing can help reduce muscular tension and maintain awareness during intimate experiences.

### Q9. What is a dry orgasm?

A dry orgasm is an orgasm experienced with little or no visible semen release. It may have different underlying physiological explanations.

### Q10. Are orgasm and ejaculation identical?

No. Orgasm is a neurological experience of climax, while ejaculation is the physical expulsion of semen. They normally occur together but can occur independently.

### Q11. What is retrograde ejaculation?

It is a condition in which semen flows backwards into the bladder rather than being expelled through the penis.

### Q12. Is retrograde ejaculation a recommended semen-retention technique?

No. It is a medical ejaculation disorder, not a recommended method of retaining semen.

Partners place their hands on one another's chest and focus on breathing, physical rhythms and emotional connection.
