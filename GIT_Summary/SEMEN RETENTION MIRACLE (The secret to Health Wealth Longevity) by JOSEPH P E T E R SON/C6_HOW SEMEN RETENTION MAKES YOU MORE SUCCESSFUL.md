## 3.2 What physical benefits does he predict?

According to this argument:

- Longer lifespan.
- Slower ageing.

## 3.3 Scientific reality check

This argument is not supported by established medical evidence.

Sperm production does require energy and nutrients, but that does not demonstrate that normal ejaculation shortens lifespan.

In fact, the medical understanding of longevity differences between men and women involves multiple interacting factors, including:

- Sex hormones.
- Genetics.
- Immune-system differences.
- Cardiovascular risks.
- Oxidative stress.
- Behavioural and environmental factors.

Research examining why women tend to live longer does not establish sperm production as the explanation. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15944465/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Furthermore, unused sperm are naturally broken down and reabsorbed by the body. This is a normal reproductive process. [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/sperm?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### My conclusion

Semen retention has not been established as a method of extending lifespan, slowing ageing or preventing physical deterioration.

This is an unsupported biological claim in the book.

## 5.3 Scientific correction

There are two separate biological facts here.

**Fact 1:** Unused sperm are naturally broken down and reabsorbed.

This is normal reproductive physiology.

**Fact 2:** There is no established evidence that retaining semen provides enough additional nutrients to increase hair growth, muscle size, intelligence, facial hair or skin quality.

Cleveland Clinic explains that sperm can remain alive in the testes for approximately 2.5 months before dying and being reabsorbed. ### Important lesson

The body's ability to reabsorb unused sperm does not mean that semen retention creates a significant nutritional advantage.
